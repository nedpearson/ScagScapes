# Scag Scapes Competitive Growth Playbook

Prepared for Charlie Mascagni  
Prepared by Ned Pearson  
Research date: September 15, 2026

## Executive recommendation

Scag should not compete for every landscape job or try to outbid large regional maintenance companies on their strongest ground.

The recommended strategy is to enter commercial accounts through a narrow, high-consequence problem:

> Measured drainage diagnosis, documented design, verified installation grade and defensible closeout records.

Then expand each account into grading, restoration, irrigation, hardscape, landscape improvements and recurring property inspections.

This gives Scag a credible reason to meet facility managers, property managers, general contractors, civil engineers, architects and public buyers without presenting itself as another commodity mowing company.

The operating system should support that strategy by connecting public opportunity monitoring, named-account pursuits, partner relationships, site assessments, proposals, production, job costing, closeout evidence and renewal work.

## What “exploit” should mean here

Use lawful public information and verified customer feedback to identify gaps in competitor positioning. Do not make unsupported claims about workmanship, safety, finances or customer service.

The ethical and effective playbook is:

1. Document what a competitor publicly emphasizes.
2. Identify an important buyer requirement they do not visibly emphasize.
3. Validate that requirement in buyer interviews and lost-deal reviews.
4. build the offer, proof and process around it.
5. Let the buyer compare evidence instead of attacking a competitor.

## Market structure

The Greater Baton Rouge market has five practical competitor types.

| Competitor type | Their natural advantage | Scag's opening |
|---|---|---|
| Regional commercial operator | Capacity, references, systems, multi-site maintenance | High-touch diagnostic pilot, specialized drainage evidence and named owner accountability |
| Full-service local landscaper | Relationships, broad service menu, established reputation | Measured diagnosis, technical documentation and performance verification |
| Landscape architect / design-build firm | Design authority, presentation quality, premium clientele | Constructability, field measurement, slope verification and execution records |
| Small drainage contractor | Lower overhead, speed, low price | Complete scope, legal discharge logic, restoration, cleanouts, warranty and proof |
| Civil / site-work contractor | Heavy equipment, public-work experience, grading capacity | Specialist subcontract role for landscape drainage, finished-site restoration and client-facing documentation |

## Commercial buyers and their real jobs

| Buyer | What they need | Scag proof |
|---|---|---|
| Property manager | Fewer complaints, fewer emergencies, clean approvals and predictable communication | Portfolio risk register, response SLA, photo record and budget phasing |
| Facility manager | Safe access, operational continuity, durable repairs and complete closeout records | Site map, job plan, QA checkpoints, as-built package and inspection schedule |
| General contractor | Reliable subcontractor, schedule discipline, clean documentation and no surprise scope gaps | Capability statement, crew plan, insurance, schedule, daily reports and change-order process |
| Civil engineer / architect | Field evidence, constructability and faithful execution | Elevation data, annotated plans, RFIs, verification records and closeout evidence |
| Public buyer | Responsive bid, compliant vendor, documented work and controlled changes | Registration, licenses, insurance, bonding plan, bid checklist, daily records and invoice support |
| Owner / developer | Fast problem resolution, asset protection and a single accountable operator | Executive summary, options, lifecycle risk and one named project lead |

## The commercial offer ladder

1. Free 20-minute qualification call
2. Paid drainage risk assessment
3. Pilot correction at one problem area or property
4. Full corrective project
5. Portfolio assessment across multiple properties
6. Annual inspection and storm-response agreement
7. Exterior property improvement program

The paid assessment protects Scag from giving away engineering labor and filters serious commercial buyers. The fee can be credited to construction when economically appropriate.

## Proposal design

Every commercial proposal should answer:

- What failed?
- What evidence supports that conclusion?
- Where should the water go?
- What constraints or permissions affect discharge?
- What work is included?
- What is excluded?
- How will Scag verify grade and performance?
- How will access, safety, erosion and restoration be managed?
- What will the client receive at closeout?
- What warranty and inspection plan applies?

Use good / better / best only when the options represent valid technical scopes. Never present an underbuilt option that Scag does not believe will work.

## How to make a cheap quote look incomplete

Place a neutral checklist in every proposal:

- Existing elevations measured
- Discharge location identified
- Discharge permission or constraint addressed
- Pipe, basin, stone and fabric specifications listed
- Required slope stated
- Utilities and access addressed
- Spoils and cleanup included
- Irrigation repair included or excluded
- Surface restoration included or excluded
- Grade verified before burial
- As-built documentation included
- Warranty and maintenance requirements stated

The buyer performs the comparison. Scag never needs to accuse another contractor.

## Named-account strategy

Create a list of 50 accounts, not 5,000 random prospects.

Recommended mix:

- 12 commercial property managers
- 8 multifamily / HOA management groups
- 8 general contractors
- 6 civil engineering firms
- 5 landscape architects
- 5 industrial or port facilities
- 3 healthcare or senior-living operators
- 3 public agencies or institutional buyers

Score each account from 0 to 5 on:

- drainage pain
- portfolio size
- project timing
- access to a decision maker
- service fit
- repeat potential
- payment quality
- strategic reference value

Do not pursue an account without a named route in. The route may be a friend, vendor, architect, engineer, property manager, general contractor, employee, association or completed-project neighbor.

## Sales discovery

Questions for a property or facility manager:

- Which sites create the most complaints after heavy rain?
- Where has the same problem been repaired more than once?
- What documentation do you need for ownership or insurance?
- Who approves capital work?
- What budget cycle applies?
- What vendor requirements apply?
- Are there discharge, easement or neighboring-property constraints?
- What would a successful pilot need to prove?

Questions after a lost deal:

- Who won?
- What did the buyer believe they did better?
- Was the deciding factor price, timing, relationship, capacity, scope or presentation?
- Was Scag included early enough to shape the scope?
- What proof was missing?
- What must change before the next comparable pursuit?

### Bid / no-bid score

Weighting:

- service fit: 20%
- decision-maker or partner access: 15%
- margin potential: 15%
- capacity and schedule: 15%
- qualification / license / bond fit: 15%
- competitive position: 10%
- repeat or reference value: 10%

Default rule: do not bid below 65 / 100 without Charlie's approval.

### Automations

- New public opportunity creates a review task, never an automatic bid.
- Due date within 14 days triggers an escalation.
- Missing mandatory document blocks submission.
- Project value above a defined threshold requires margin and cash-flow approval.
- Loss automatically creates a structured buyer-interview task.
- Completed commercial project creates a closeout package, case-study draft and renewal date.
